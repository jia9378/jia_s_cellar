CDEFGAB

A ring-based glove that turns the movement of each finger into a piano note, so playing happens in the air rather than on keys.

Concept

Why a glove

Piano keys fix the hand in one place. I wanted the note to follow the finger instead, which means the instrument has to read the body directly. The question that drove the whole project was how little hardware you can put on a hand and still tell which finger moved.

Coming from jewelry, the constraint that mattered most was that whatever sits on the fingers should feel like a ring, not a device.

First attempt

sEMG on the forearm

Three weeks went into a surface-EMG system reading two channels on the forearm. Offline it classified fingers at 81.6%. In the live test it fell to 52% and, more importantly, it could not tell when a press happened.

81.6% → 52%

Offline accuracy against live accuracy, five fingers, 25 trials.

Challenge and pivot

To IMU sensors

Three papers confirmed the gap was hardware, not code: forearm EMG blurs adjacent fingers. So I moved the sensing onto the fingers themselves, one small IMU at the base of each.

Placing sensors at the base rather than the tip keeps the fingertips and joints free, so the hand moves naturally while the system still captures each finger on its own.

Development

Classification

With a sensor per finger the problem changes from classification to peak detection. A press shows up as a sharp rise in gyro energy, and the finger is simply whichever channel is loudest at the peak.

For wearability I swapped to smaller IMU boards and a round I2C multiplexer that sits flatter on the back of the hand.

Web socket

Arduino → Python → browser

In the final version the Arduino only reads the IMUs, computes gyro energy, and streams it. Peak detection, finger selection, cooldown, and velocity live in Python, which sends structured JSON to the browser over WebSocket. That makes thresholds easy to tune without reflashing. 

// Arduino: read, reduce, stream
float raw = abs(dgx) + abs(dgy) + abs(dgz);
Serial.println(raw);
# Python: decide
if v > PEAK_THRESHOLD:
    state[i] = TRACKING
elif v < tracked_peak[i] * DROP_CONFIRM:
    state[i] = FIRED
    ws.send(json.dumps({"finger": i, "velocity": v}))

Hardware

CAD and CAM

The rings are the part of the project I care most about being right. Each housing is sized to the finger it sits on and holds the board so the wire leaves toward the back of the hand rather than across the knuckle.

Outcome

Five fingers, five notes, no keyboard. Next steps are chord detection from simultaneous peaks and moving the rings from PLA to cast metal.
